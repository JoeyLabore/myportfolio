Process

01 / 06 — Understand context, behaviour, systems, and constraints

Before defining solutions, I focus on understanding the current state: users, stakeholders, workflows, systems, incentives, technical constraints, and business context.

Methods vary depending on the problem, from interviews and audits to journey mapping, analytics, ecosystem mapping, and workflow analysis. The goal is simple: reduce assumptions and understand how things actually work.

02 / 06 — Define the real problem

Most problems are symptoms. Before designing, I work to identify root causes, align business goals, define success criteria, and prioritise where effort will create the greatest value.

This stage turns research into decisions: what problem are we solving, for whom, and how will we know if it worked?

03 / 06 — Future-state thinking

Rather than jumping into polished interfaces, I explore how workflows, systems, and decision-making could work better. Product, engineering, stakeholders, and users help shape future-state thinking early to reduce ambiguity later.

Outputs may include flows, service models, concepts, wireframes, prototypes, or lightweight AI-assisted explorations. Whatever best helps teams collaborate and align around direction.

04 / 06 — De-risk before build

Before committing resources, I validate assumptions, test concepts, and refine scope. Validation helps teams understand what creates value, what creates friction, and where to further focus effort.

Depending on the product, this may involve usability testing, behavioural analysis, further prioritisation, production-like prototypes, and stakeholder alignment.

05 / 06 — Turn ideas into scalable products

I translate validated concepts into production-ready experiences through design systems, reusable patterns, final designs, implementation guidance, and close collaboration with engineering.

Quality is part of delivery. Accessibility, responsiveness, interaction behaviour, and implementation fidelity are reviewed throughout the process to ensure products scale effectively while balancing speed, quality, and maintainability.

06 / 06 — Iterate, learn, and improve

Products are never finished. After launch, I use qualitative insight, behavioural data, analytics, QA, and operational feedback to understand what is working, what is creating friction, and where iteration creates the greatest value.

The goal is continuous improvement rather than one-time delivery.
