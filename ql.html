Problem

A fragmented experience that reduced trust

Quicken Loans, part of Rocket Companies, was operating with a fragmented and outdated digital experience. Years of design and technical debt had created inconsistencies across product, web, and marketing touchpoints, particularly within high-intent journeys such as loan eligibility, rate exploration, calculators, and loan applications.

The issue extended beyond usability. In high-stakes financial decisions, trust is shaped by clarity and consistency. Fragmented interactions and inconsistent patterns created friction at critical moments, reducing trust, confidence and making the experience difficult to navigate.

Internally, the absence of shared foundations slowed delivery, increased duplication, and made it harder for teams to improve or scale the experience consistently.

Solution

Rebuilding clarity, trust, and consistency

Rather than approaching the challenge as a website redesign, we reframed it as a product, brand, and systems problem. Research and current-state analysis showed the experience lacked consistency across journeys, interaction patterns, content, and tone of voice, reducing trust and increasing friction.

We redesigned key journeys to improve clarity, simplify decision-making, and create a more cohesive experience across touchpoints. At the same time, we refreshed the brand and tone of voice to feel clearer, more approachable, and more relevant to modern homebuyers.

To support long-term consistency and delivery, we introduced reusable foundations through a shared design system, enabling teams to scale improvements, strengthen accessibility, and ship more efficiently.

Impact

01. A clearer, more trustworthy experience

Simplifying key journeys reduced friction during high-stakes decision moments, helping users better understand options and navigate the mortgage process with greater clarity and confidence.

02. Stronger engagement in high-intent journeys

Aligning navigation, content, and calls-to-action more closely with user intent improved the relevance and usability of high-traffic experiences, particularly within learning and rate exploration journeys.

03. Clearer paths from exploration to action

By connecting educational content more closely to decision-making journeys, we reduced friction between learning and action and better supported progression through key flows.

04. Greater consistency across touchpoints

A shared system aligned product, marketing, and content experiences, creating a more cohesive platform with clearer interaction patterns, content structure, and brand expression.

05. Faster delivery through shared foundations

Reusable patterns and a shared design system reduced duplication, improved collaboration across teams, and created stronger foundations for scalable product delivery.

01 Understanding the Users

Understanding the modern homebuyer

Before defining direction, we needed to understand how modern homebuyers approached high-stakes financial decisions and where trust broke down across the mortgage journey.

I partnered with Rocket’s market research team to conduct qualitative interviews across four user profiles identified through previous initiatives. We spoke with 18 participants who were either actively buying a home or had recently completed the process.

The goal was not simply to identify usability issues, but to understand how people navigated uncertainty, evaluated options, and made decisions around loan eligibility, rate exploration, and financing. These conversations revealed the emotional, behavioural, and informational factors influencing confidence throughout the journey and helped identify where the experience was creating friction.

Interview synthesis for Profile 1

User Research Key Takeaways

01. Trust is low across digital tools and professionals

Users were sceptical of both digital platforms and real estate professionals, citing hidden costs, unclear information, and inconsistent communication. Trust was fragile and easily undermined when information felt incomplete or inconsistent.

02. Decision-making felt complex and fragmented

Scattered information, unclear pathways, and inconsistent guidance made the mortgage journey difficult to navigate. Users struggled to build confidence in decisions around costs, timelines, eligibility, and next steps.

03. Financial uncertainty is the biggest barrier

Affordability concerns, debt, and fluctuating interest rates created hesitation throughout the journey. Many users delayed decisions until they felt more financially secure or better informed.

04. Decisions are both emotional and practical

Mortgage decisions combined financial logic with emotional goals such as stability, family, and long-term security. Despite growing digital expectations, users still wanted human reassurance during high-stakes moments.

05. The brand lacked clarity and differentiation

Users recognised Quicken Loans but struggled to understand what the brand stood for, how it differed from Rocket, or why they should trust it. Familiarity existed, but clarity and confidence did not.

02 Understanding Current State

A fragmented experience

To understand why trust was breaking down, we audited the experience across product, web, and marketing touchpoints. What emerged was not a collection of isolated usability issues, but a fragmented system shaped by years of design and technical debt.

Inconsistencies appeared across interaction patterns, typography, colour, components, navigation, and tone of voice, alongside accessibility gaps and structural issues. The experience felt uneven and poorly maintained, creating friction in moments where clarity and confidence mattered most.

In financial decision-making, trust is reinforced through consistency. When interactions feel fragmented or information appears inconsistent, users begin to question both the product and the guidance it provides.

03 Refreshing the brand

Not a new brand, just a more honest one

Research showed that users recognised Quicken Loans but struggled to understand what the brand stood for, how it differed from Rocket, or why they should trust it. Familiarity existed, but clarity did not. While the brand was recognisable, it felt rooted in the past and disconnected from the expectations of modern homebuyers.

Rather than reinventing the brand, we made a deliberate decision to evolve what already existed. Quicken Loans had familiarity and recognition, but lacked a clear voice, personality, and consistent expression across touchpoints.

Brandscape Exploration

We used that familiarity as a foundation, shifting the brand toward something clearer, more approachable, and more human. Instead of feeling overly polished or corporate, the goal was to feel honest, relatable, and consistent, building trust through clarity, tone, and recognisable experience patterns.

04 Key Journey Redesign

Redesigning key journeys through research and behavioural insights

Rather than redesigning the experience uniformly, we prioritised the moments where users showed the highest intent but encountered the greatest friction. Research, current-state analysis, analytics, and behavioural data helped identify where users struggled to move from exploration to action.

Heatmaps and engagement data showed strong interaction with learning content, mortgage rates, and calculators, but weak progression through key journeys. Navigation friction, unclear hierarchy, and a disconnected brand made it harder for users to understand next steps or trust the brand and move forward confidently.

User engagement and friction hotspots

These insights helped prioritise high-impact opportunities. We redesigned journeys such as learning centre, rate exploration, and lender contact flows by simplifying entry points, clarifying hierarchy, improving content structure, and better aligning experiences with user intent.

05 An Accessible Experience

Making the experience accessible by design

Accessibility was treated as a foundational part of the redesign rather than being addressed later in the process. The existing experience lacked consistent structure, making journeys harder to navigate and unreliable for assistive technologies.

Rather than addressing isolated issues, we introduced clearer semantic structure, interaction patterns, and accessibility standards across the experience. Focus order, reading order, landmarks, ARIA labelling, colour contrast, typography, and interaction states were designed to support clearer navigation, better comprehension, and more predictable interactions.

These patterns were embedded into the design system so accessibility could scale consistently across journeys rather than rely on page-by-page fixes. In addition to improving usability for more users, stronger semantic structure reinforced content hierarchy and improved discoverability through better site structure and SEO.

06 Design System

Building a reusable design system

We focused on building a system, not just redesigning pages. Using an atomic design approach, we created reusable foundations, components, and patterns that could scale across the product.

The system was designed to support multiple teams, from developers to content editors, allowing them to create pages quickly within the CMS while maintaining consistency. This improved development speed, reduced duplication, and helped standardise content for better SEO performance.

Colour Variable Structure

Typography Variable Structure

We structured the system using primitives and tokens. Base values, like colour and typography, were defined as primitives and then mapped to semantic tokens used across components.

This separation allowed us to scale and maintain the system more effectively. Global changes could be applied quickly, while still allowing for targeted updates at the token level, such as adjusting a primary CTA without impacting the entire system.

Typgraphy Variables

Spacing Variables

We introduced a consistent spacing system and responsive grid to bring structure and alignment across the product. Spacing tokens standardised padding and layout relationships, while the grid system ensured layouts scaled seamlessly across breakpoints.

With a large portion of users on mobile, this allowed us to design responsively from the start, maintaining clarity and usability across all screen sizes.

Grids

Components were documented with clear specifications, including spacing, behaviour, states, and accessibility. This created a shared reference for both design and development.

We partnered closely with engineering to ensure components were implemented consistently, reducing ambiguity and speeding up handoff. This made it easier for teams to reuse patterns and scale the system across the product.
