Project Background

Toyota Racing Development (TRD) is Toyota’s motorsport division, building and supporting high-performance Nascar race engines.

Operating in a highly regulated environment, performance-driven environment, TRD coordinates engineering, manufacturing, testing, and track support to deliver engines under tight timelines.


  • Client: Toyota Racing Development
  • Type of work: Enterprise UX
  • Duration: 2025 - Present
  • Role: Product Design Lead. I led the redesign of core workflows and platform experiences.

Problem & Solution

Problem

TRD’s 20+ year-old process became complex and fragmented, relying on tribal knowledge, lacking standardised workflows, and limiting lifecycle visibility. This led to data inconsistencies and increased operational risk.


Solution

Transformed their platform into a structured, workflow-driven system, standardising operations, improving lifecycle visibility, and reducing reliance on manual processes and tribal knowledge.

  • Future state workflows

  • Design system integrated with AI development workflows

Note on confidentiality

This case study reflects work conducted across a complex internal ecosystem. To respect confidentiality, certain visuals and details have been simplified, anonymised, or omitted. The content presented here focuses on communicating my process, thinking, and key outcomes.

Problem

From spreadsheets to systems

TRD operates in a complex, performance-critical environment supported by thier Internal System, its core operational platform. Over time, however, the system became fragmented.

Much of the work shifted outside it, into Excel, shared folders, and manual processes, creating a disconnected, error-prone workflow with no clear source of truth.

Soluton

Structuring complex workflows

The solution wasn’t just to improve usability, it was to re-establish thier Internal System as the operational backbone of TRD by simplifying workflows, reconnecting work happening outside the system, reducing manual overhead, and building a scalable UI and design system for long-term growth.

01 Define the Ways of Working

Before the Solution, the Shift

To support the redesign, we defined a structured way of working that aligned product, design, and engineering across the full lifecycle, from discovery through delivery and post-release iteration.

Way of Working Framework

A key challenge was aligning expectations around how the solution would be built. TRD was used to a model where internal teams implemented requests quickly and moved on, contributing to fragmented workflows through patchwork fixes.

Rather than simply building what was requested, we intentionally reduced scope and focused on high-impact problems first. The solution was developed iteratively, expanding over time as new needs emerged.

This approach introduced a more structured, problem-led way of working, balancing speed with long-term scalability.

02 Understanding Current State

Understanding the System

It was critical to understand how work actually happened across TRD, not just within thier Internal System, but across the broader ecosystem of tools, teams, and processes.

The system extended beyond a single platform, with key workflows distributed across in-person conversations, separate Excel files, and documentation. To understand these processes, we conducted stakeholder and user interviews, capturing insights through journey maps, user flows, object models, and system diagrams.

Initial Product Diagram

Key Insights

01. The system wasn’t the source of truth

Critical workflows existed outside of thier Internal System, in Excels, shared folders, and informal coordination, creating inconsistencies and making the system difficult to trust.

02. Workflows were fragmented across teams and tools

Processes spanned multiple systems with unclear ownership, leading to communication breakdowns and a heavy reliance on manual handoffs.

03. Users relied on tribal knowledge to complete tasks

The system exposed complexity without guidance, requiring users to depend on experience rather than structured workflows. Built over 20 years with the same leadership, the system was deeply embedded in institutional knowledge, making it difficult for new users to onboard.

04. Errors were caught too late in the process

Validation occurred primarily during review and approval stages, relying heavily on manual checks. As a result, errors introduced through human oversight often propagated across the system before being identified.

05. Delivery was driven by requests, not problems

Request-driven implementation led to patchwork solutions, addressing immediate needs while increasing long-term system complexity.

04 Explore future state

Designing from the Flow

Building on these insights, we explored how the system could evolve to better support decision-making, planning, and operational workflows.

A key principle was to define future-state flows first, often co-creating them with stakeholders, and align on them before moving into visual design. In some cases, early visualisation helped communicate ideas, but the priority was always establishing shared understanding and agreement on how the system should work.

Future State Decision Flow: Engine Replacement

After defining the flows, we facilitated co-creation sessions with stakeholders and users, collaboratively shaping high-level wireframes for key moments in the experience.

Working at a lower level of fidelity created space to explore ideas without the pressure of visual polish. This allowed us to focus on structure and information hierarchy, gaining early alignment on how the system should work before moving into detailed design.

05 Initial Prototypes & Screens

Designing with Real Data

With aligned flows in place, we began translating ideas into testable concepts.

Given the complexity and importance of data, low-fidelity designs were often insufficient, dummy content made it difficult for stakeholders to interpret the system and provide meaningful feedback.

To address this, we moved quickly into high-fidelity prototypes using realistic data. This enabled more accurate evaluation, allowing stakeholders and users to engage with the designs as they would in real scenarios, and ensuring feedback was both informed and actionable.

06 Validate & Refine the Solution

From AI Prototypes to Real-World Validation

With high-fidelity concepts in place, we shifted into validation, testing ideas through direct collaboration with stakeholders and users.

Onsite co-creation session

To support this, we leveraged AI-driven prototyping tools such as Figma MCP and Figma Make to create production-like experiences with realistic data. This allowed us to move beyond static designs, building interactive prototypes that better reflected how the system would behave in practice.

07 Final Design & Refinement

From Insights to Final Design

Building on validated insights, we translated learnings from co-creation and user testing into the final design. Feedback from stakeholders and users directly informed how workflows were structured, how information was surfaced, and how the system responded to real-world scenarios.

08 Design to Delivery

AI Driven Development

A key part of handoff was defining and establishing a scalable design system within Figma, creating a consistent foundation of components, tokens, and patterns that could be reused across the platform.

To bridge the gap between design and engineering, we explored new workflows using Figma’s MCP and Codex, enabling design decisions and components to be translated more directly into code. These outputs were then integrated into the BMAD method, supporting a more structured, AI-assisted development approach. By aligning the design system with development workflows, we ensured that designs could be efficiently & accurately built, creating a tighter connection between design and code.

01 - Documented variables from primitives to tokens, creating a scalable foundation for components and design aligned with atomic design principles.

02- Translated the design system architecture, from primitives and tokens to atoms and pages, into code using Figma MCP and Codex, building a reusable component library in Storybook.

03 - Delivered Figma files and the component library as context for the BMAD method, enabling structured, AI-assisted implementation.
