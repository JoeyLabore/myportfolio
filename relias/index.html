Problem

Adapting to a Rapidly Changing Landscape

As generative AI rapidly reshaped content production, Relias faced a critical challenge: evolve existing workflows or risk being outpaced by faster, AI-native competitors.

While Relias's writers were already experimenting with tools like ChatGPT, usage remained fragmented and disconnected from existing operational processes. This gap between AI capability and content operations created inefficiencies, inconsistencies, and growing pressure to scale production without compromising quality, compliance, or accuracy.

Solution

A Unified Content System

To help Relias transition from fragmented AI experimentation to an integrated operational workflow, we designed a centralised AI-native Content Studio platform.

AI was embedded across writing, review, and production workflows, supporting the business goal of reducing transcript production time by 50% while maintaining quality and compliance standards.

Beyond AI integration, the platform unified previously disconnected systems and workflows into a shared operational environment, improving visibility, reducing duplication, and streamlining collaboration across teams.

Impact

01. FROM MANUAL DRAFTING TO AI-FIRST CONTENT CREATION

AI-first generation significantly reduced transcript production time while maintaining quality, compliance, and editorial standards.

02. FROM FRAGMENTED TOOLS TO UNIFIED CONTENT WORKFLOWS

Consolidating disconnected systems into a shared operational platform reduced context switching and improved visibility across writing, review, and QA.

03. FROM MANUAL REVIEW TO STRUCTURED CONTENT GOVERNANCE

Embedded standards, validation logic, and workflow guidance improved consistency while reducing preventable formatting and review issues.

04. FROM LOW TRUST TO TRANSPARENT AI OPERATIONS

Making AI generation, edits, and sources visible improved confidence, accountability, and traceability across content workflows.

05. FROM RAPID PRODUCT DELIVERY TO SCALABLE SYSTEM FOUNDATIONS

A scalable design system and unified platform foundation allowed teams to build and iterate more efficiently, supporting ongoing product growth.

01 Understanding Current State

Uncovering a System in Transition

We conducted a four-week discovery phase through on-site workshops and stakeholder interviews with writers, reviewers, and leadership teams to understand how content creation workflows were evolving in practice.

What emerged wasn’t a clearly broken system, existing processes were functional. Instead, we uncovered a workflow ecosystem already beginning to shift around generative AI. Teams were informally integrating tools like ChatGPT into writing and review processes, creating new opportunities, inconsistencies, and operational risks outside existing systems.

Rather than solving a visible UX problem, we were defining how a working system could evolve into a more integrated AI-native workflow.

Key Insights

01. HIGHLY MANUAL AND REPETITIVE WORK

Teams spent significant time copying, reformatting, and re-entering information across disconnected tools instead of creating and validating content, slowing production and introducing preventable errors.

02. DISCONNECTED TOOLS AND FRAGMENTED WORKFLOWS

Content operations spanned multiple systems with poor integration, creating fragmented handoffs, lost context, and inefficiencies across the production lifecycle.

03. INEFFICIENT REVIEW, QA, AND VERSIONING

Review workflows relied heavily on manual coordination. Feedback was difficult to track, multiple review layers create bottlenecks, and preventable issues repeatedly surfaced during QA.

04. INCONSISTENT CONTENT QUALITY, ALIGNMENT, AND BRAND VOICE

There was no system-level enforcement of learning objectives, content standards, or brand consistency, relying instead on manual review and institutional knowledge.

05. LIMITED TRACEABILITY AND TRUST IN AI-ASSISTED CONTENT

Teams lacked visibility into how content was generated, edited, and validated, creating compliance risks and reducing confidence in AI-assisted workflows.

03 Explore future state

Shifting Mindsets

It was critical to involve users directly in shaping how content operations could evolve within an AI-native system.

Early conversations naturally focused on improving existing workflows rather than rethinking them entirely. To move beyond incremental change, we reframed discussions around the future: how responsibilities would shift, how AI could support decision-making, and how content workflows could operate within a more integrated system.

One recurring challenge was maintaining content structure, quality, and consistency. Writers relied heavily on manually applied Word-based templates for formatting and standards, creating inconsistencies across outputs. This revealed an opportunity to embed structure and guidance directly into the system, reducing manual overhead while improving consistency by design.

Collaborative Workshop Session Board

Before moving into interface design, it was essential to align on how the system should function at an operational level. Using insights gathered through workshops and discovery, we developed future-state workflows, journey maps, and system diagrams to model how content, responsibilities, and AI-supported processes could evolve across the platform.

In parallel, we explored how user roles themselves would evolve, shifting from content creators toward editors, validators, and decision-makers operating within AI-first workflows.

04 Initial Prototypes & Screens

Showing Less, Enabling More

We explored a wide range of interface concepts to support writing, research, and review workflows across the platform.

A key breakthrough emerged when we realised many traditionally separate interface patterns, searching, generating suggestions, editing content, and workflow actions, could be consolidated into a single conversational interaction model. With AI acting as a contextual assistant, users no longer needed to navigate multiple UI components to complete tasks. Instead, they could express intent directly within the workflow.

This shift reduced interface complexity, streamlined operational workflows, and significantly simplified the frontend architecture, creating a system that was easier to use, build, and scale.

05 Validate & Refine the Solution

Testing Beyond the Interface with AI Prototypes

Traditional Figma prototypes were not sufficient for evaluating AI-native interaction patterns. Static screens could not capture how users would engage with a system driven by conversation, intent, and dynamic responses.

To simulate realistic interactions, I built a production-like prototype using Figma MCP and lightweight AI agents integrated through OpenAI’s API. This allowed us to test how users interacted with conversational workflows in context, rather than evaluating isolated interface states.

Production Prototype Testing Session

These prototypes enabled us to move beyond traditional usability testing and observe how users phrased requests, navigated workflows conversationally, and formed expectations around system behaviour and responsiveness.

The testing also revealed important implementation insights, helping shape how AI agents, orchestration logic, and conversational workflows should be structured within the product.

06 Final Design & Refinement

Aligning design with real system behaviour

To design AI-native workflows effectively, it was essential to understand how orchestration systems, retrieval pipelines, validation layers, and specialised agents shaped behaviour behind the interface. Working closely with AI architects and engineers, we mapped how these systems coordinated content generation, review, and decision-making processes across the platform.

LO Orchestration Architecture

Understanding these orchestration flows directly informed how conversational workflows, interaction states, and decision-making patterns were designed within the product.

07 Design to Delivery

Designing for Scale

To support rapid product delivery, we developed an initial system foundation that enabled the team to move quickly during the first four-month implementation phase.

As the platform evolved, this foundation expanded into a scalable design system built around reusable components, tokens, and structured interaction patterns. Using atomic design principles, we established a system that ensured consistency across conversational workflows, reduced implementation overhead, and supported the growing complexity of AI-native product experiences.

This approach allowed the team to balance rapid iteration with long-term scalability, creating a product foundation capable of evolving alongside both the platform and its orchestration systems.

08 POST-LAUNCH EVOLUTION

Continuous Iteration

Because the platform was designed and delivered within a rapid five-week discovery and four-month implementation timeline, continuous iteration was always part of the strategy. Rather than attempting to solve every edge case upfront, the goal was to establish a scalable foundation that could evolve through real-world usage and operational feedback.

Following launch, we conducted additional user interviews and testing sessions to understand how workflows were performing in practice. Some interaction patterns validated earlier assumptions, while others revealed new friction points, evolving mental models, and opportunities for refinement as users adapted to AI-native workflows.

These insights were prioritised based on operational impact, implementation complexity, and long-term workflow value. This allowed the team to focus on refining high-impact behaviours while continuing to evolve the platform incrementally over time.

This is an active, evolving project.

The platform continues to evolve through ongoing stakeholder and user feedback, operational refinement, and the expansion of AI-native workflows. This case study will continue to grow alongside it.
